How to Use

  1. Type your video topic in a few plain words — “adopting a rescue dog”, “budget travel in Japan”, “building a gaming PC”. Short, natural topics produce the cleanest titles; the generator handles the framing, the numbers and the curiosity angles for you.
  2. Click “Generate 10 titles.” Ten titles appear in proven formats: “I Tried {topic} for 7 Days”, “7 Mistakes Everyone Makes”, honest reviews, how-to guides and open loops that make the click feel necessary.
  3. Check the character count on each title. Every result shows its length, and titles are automatically trimmed to 70 characters or fewer where possible — the range where a title survives search results, suggested sidebar and mobile feeds without being cut mid-thought.
  4. Copy your favorite — or blend two. Take the curiosity frame from one title and the specific number from another. The generator gives you angles; your knowledge of the actual video picks the honest combination.
  5. Generate again before you decide. Each click draws a different ten from the template pool. Brainstorming twenty or thirty angles costs a minute and is the cheapest click-through-rate improvement a creator can make.

Title Tips That Raise Click-Through Rate

  • Front-load what matters. On mobile, viewers may see only the first 40–50 characters. Put the topic keyword and the tension at the start (“Rescue Dog: 5 Things Nobody Tells You”), and let supporting words trail at the end where a cutoff hurts least.
  • One idea per title. A title trying to promise a tutorial, a story and a review at once promises nothing clearly. Each generated title carries a single angle — keep it that way when you edit, and save the other angles for the description and pinned comment.
  • Numbers earn their place. Specific numbers (7 mistakes, 24 hours, $0 vs $100) signal a structured, finite video and consistently lift clicks. Odd and precise numbers feel more honest than round ones. Only use a number your video actually contains.
  • Title and thumbnail are a team, not twins. If the thumbnail says “I QUIT”, the title should not repeat “I Quit My Job” — it should add the missing piece: “…After 10 Years (What Happened Next)”. Together they form one complete curiosity loop; duplicated, they waste half your click surface.
  • Never promise what the video cannot pay off. YouTube measures viewer satisfaction, not just clicks. A title that oversells produces fast exits, and fast exits teach the algorithm to stop suggesting your videos. The strongest titles sit exactly at the edge of what the video delivers.
  • Use the year sparingly. Adding “2026” helps search-driven topics that genuinely update (tools, prices, rules, reviews). On stories, challenges and entertainment, a year stamp makes evergreen content look dated and quietly lowers clicks over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

Aim for 70 characters or fewer — that is why this generator trims to that range and shows a count on every result. YouTube allows up to 100 characters, but search results and mobile feeds truncate long before that, often around 40–50 characters on phones. If the interesting part sits past the cutoff, it effectively does not exist.

Do keywords in the title still matter for YouTube search?

Yes. The title is one of the strongest relevance signals YouTube has, especially for newer channels without watch-history data. Put your main topic words naturally near the front of the title — the generator does this automatically by building titles around your exact topic phrase — and support them in the description.

Can I use this title generator for YouTube Shorts?

Yes. Shorts titles follow the same curiosity and clarity rules, but shorter is even better because Shorts are browsed in a fast feed. Generate ten, then pick the punchiest short option, and put extra keywords in the Short's description with two or three hashtags including #shorts.

Why does every title show a character count?

Because length decides whether your title survives being displayed. A brilliant 95-character title that gets truncated to “I Tried Adopting a Rescue Dog for 7 Days — Here's Wha…” loses its payoff. The count lets you compare titles on equal footing and keep the full promise visible everywhere the video appears.

Should I copy the title style of big creators in my niche?

Study their structure, never copy their exact titles. Notice how top channels in your niche use numbers, open loops and stakes — those patterns are what this generator is built on. Then write titles in your own words for your own footage. Imitated titles read as derivative, and YouTube viewers are quick to sense it.
