How to Use the Best Time to Post

This tool collects the best posting windows for a US audience on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube, based on the patterns large creator studies see again and again — and it shows you the best days for each platform too. Used well, timing will not save a weak video, but it reliably gives a good video a bigger first hour. Here is how to use it.

  1. Tap your platform at the top — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or YouTube. The panel updates instantly with that platform's three strongest posting windows.
  2. Read the windows in Eastern Time. Each row shows the moment people scroll, the exact window, and why it works, so you understand the pattern instead of just copying a time blindly.
  3. Check the best-days pills under the table. On most platforms Tuesday to Thursday win on weekdays, while YouTube peaks across the weekend — plan your strongest content for those days.
  4. Post 30 to 60 minutes before the window opens. Your content needs a little engagement momentum before the peak arrives, so it is already moving when the big scroll wave hits.
  5. Test for two weeks, then trust your own analytics. These windows are the best general starting point available; your own account's audience graph is the final word once you have about a month of data.

Viral Tips: Timing Tricks Serious Creators Use

  • Consistency beats the perfect minute. Posting at a good time every day grows an account faster than posting at the perfect time twice a month — algorithms reward accounts that reliably show up for their audience.
  • Win the first hour. Reply to every comment in the first sixty minutes after posting. Early replies create comment threads, and threads are one of the strongest engagement signals on every platform.
  • Mind the time-zone trap. If your audience is spread across the US, Eastern Time windows reach the East Coast live and the West Coast within the same evening — which is why this tool standardises everything on ET.
  • Give short videos a head start. TikTok and Reels need time to be tested on a small audience before they can spread, so post shorts a full hour before the peak rather than at the peak itself.
  • Do not post your best idea into your worst slot. Save experimental content for off-peak tests, and put proven formats — the ones your audience already loves — into your prime windows.
  • Stack your platforms. Publish on YouTube in the afternoon window, then share the link to your Instagram and Facebook stories the same evening to feed the crucial first 24 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single best time to post on Instagram?

For a US audience, weekday lunch (11 AM – 1 PM ET) and weekday evenings (7 PM – 9 PM ET) are the most reliable Instagram windows, with Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday the strongest days. Post about half an hour before the window opens, so your Reel is already gathering likes when the peak begins.

Is the best time different for TikTok and YouTube Shorts?

Yes, slightly. TikTok's biggest US wave is 6 PM – 9 PM ET on weekday evenings, and videos should go up about an hour early because TikTok tests them on a small audience first. YouTube Shorts ride the same evening wave, while long YouTube videos do best published at 2 PM – 4 PM ET on weekdays.

Are these posting times guaranteed to make a post go viral?

No — timing is a multiplier, not a guarantee. A strong hook and good watch time matter more than the clock. What timing does is make sure your best content meets the biggest possible audience in its first hour, which is exactly when platforms decide how far to push it.

How often should I post?

For most small creators, one quality post a day on short-form platforms and two to three long videos a week on YouTube is a sustainable rhythm. If you must choose, choose consistency: a schedule you can keep for six months beats a burst you abandon in three weeks.

My audience is not in the US. Should I still use these times?

Use the same pattern, shifted to your audience's time zone: lunch scrolls, after-dinner evenings and relaxed weekend mornings behave similarly almost everywhere. Then confirm with your own analytics, which show exactly when your followers are online.
