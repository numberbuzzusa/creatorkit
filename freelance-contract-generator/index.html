How to Use the Freelance Contract Generator

  1. Name both parties properly. Use the legal business name if either side works through an LLC or company — that is who is bound by the contract.
  2. Write the scope like a checklist. List exactly what will be delivered (pages, files, formats, rounds of edits). Anything not listed is not included — that single habit prevents most scope-creep arguments.
  3. Pin down the money. Fixed fee or hourly rate, when each payment is due, and how many revision rounds are included. A 50% deposit before work starts is standard for fixed-fee projects.
  4. Keep the IP clause. The template transfers ownership of the final deliverables when the client has paid in full — the freelancer's built-in protection against non-payment.
  5. Sign before you start. Download the PDF and have both parties sign and date it. A signed contract beats a long email thread every time.

Clauses Worth Understanding

  • Change orders. Extra work must be agreed in writing before it starts — otherwise neither side can prove what was promised.
  • Termination. Either side can end the agreement with written notice, and the client pays for work completed to that date.
  • Independent contractor status. The clause confirms the freelancer is not an employee, which matters for taxes and benefits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this freelance contract really free?

Yes — completely free, no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser so your client details and rates never leave your device.

Do I need a contract for a small freelance job?

For anything beyond a trivial favor, yes. A one-page agreement covering scope, price and payment timing prevents the two most common freelance problems: unpaid invoices and endless revisions.

When does the client own the work?

Under this template, ownership of the final deliverables transfers when the client has paid in full. Before that, the work remains the freelancer's property.

Can I use this contract in any state?

The template lets you pick the governing state. It is a general-purpose template, not legal advice — for large engagements or regulated industries, have an attorney review it.
