How to Use the NDA Generator

  1. Pick the right type. One-way when only one side shares sensitive information (for example, you pitch your idea to a company). Mutual when both sides will see each other's private information — the most common setup between businesses.
  2. Write a real purpose. The purpose clause limits how the information may be used. Be specific enough to be meaningful: "evaluating a possible marketing engagement" beats "business purposes."
  3. Choose a fair term. Two to five years is typical for business information. Trade secrets stay protected as long as they remain secret, regardless of the term you pick.
  4. Sign before you share. The NDA only protects information disclosed under it — have both parties sign and date the PDF before the meeting, deck, or data room.

What a Fair NDA Always Includes

  • Standard exclusions. Information that is already public, already known, or independently developed is never covered — if an NDA you are handed lacks these, ask why.
  • No license granted. Seeing confidential material does not give the receiver any right to use the underlying intellectual property.
  • Return of materials. When the purpose ends, the information goes back or is destroyed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this NDA really free?

Yes — completely free with no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser so the names and purpose you enter never leave your device.

What is the difference between a one-way and a mutual NDA?

In a one-way NDA, one party discloses and the other promises to keep it secret. In a mutual NDA, both parties disclose and both are bound. Choose mutual whenever information flows in both directions.

How long should an NDA last?

Two to five years from disclosure is standard for ordinary business information. Trade secrets are protected for as long as they remain secret under the law.

Does an NDA protect my idea?

An NDA protects the confidential information you share under it — it does not patent an idea or stop someone from independently developing something similar. It is a general template, not legal advice.
