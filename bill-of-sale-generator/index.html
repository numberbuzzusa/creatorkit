How to Use the Bill of Sale Generator

  1. Use full legal names and addresses. The bill of sale is the buyer's proof of ownership — names should match the IDs and, for vehicles, the title.
  2. Describe the item precisely. For general items, note brand, model, serial number, and condition. Details here prevent "that's not the one I sold" disputes.
  3. For vehicles, switch on the vehicle fields. Enter the year, make, model, VIN exactly as shown on the title, and the odometer reading — federal law requires the seller's mileage statement on most vehicle transfers.
  4. Decide on "as-is." Most private sales are as-is: the buyer accepts the item in its current condition with no warranty. It is checked by default; uncheck it only if the seller is actually giving a written warranty.
  5. Sign two copies at the exchange. Both parties sign and date, each keeps a copy, and the buyer uses theirs for title transfer and registration.

Good to Know

  • A bill of sale is not a title. For vehicles, the signed title transfers ownership at the state office — the bill of sale is your supporting proof of the deal's price and date, which matters for sales tax.
  • Keep it with your records. Sellers: your copy proves you no longer own the vehicle if tickets or tolls arrive later. Buyers: yours proves what you paid.
  • Some states have official forms. A few states require or offer their own vehicle bill of sale form — check your state's DMV if you are transferring a vehicle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this bill of sale really free?

Yes — completely free with no sign-up, and everything runs in your browser so the names, VIN and price you enter never leave your device.

What does 'as-is' mean?

The buyer accepts the item in its current condition and the seller gives no warranty about it. It is the standard term for private-party sales of used vehicles and equipment.

Do I need a bill of sale to sell a car?

In most states the signed title is the legal transfer document, but a bill of sale is strongly recommended — it records the price (used to calculate sales tax), the date, and the odometer statement federal law requires.

Does a bill of sale need to be notarized?

Most states do not require notarization for a private bill of sale, though a few do for vehicle transfers. The template includes an optional witness line either way. It is a general template, not legal advice.
