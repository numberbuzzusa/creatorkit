How to Use the Roofing Cost Calculator

  1. Pick your city. The calculator loads that city's researched installed rate per square foot and its real permit fee range — a roof in New York is priced from New York data, not a national average.
  2. Enter your home's living area and stories. Roof area is estimated from your footprint (living area ÷ stories) times a pitch factor, the same method published cost calculators use. A 2,000 sq ft one-story home has roughly 2,300 sq ft of roof at a standard pitch.
  3. Choose pitch and material. Steeper roofs need more material and slower, safer labor. Material is the biggest single lever: 3-tab shingles run about 22% below architectural, while metal is roughly 1.9× and tile about 2.3× the architectural price.
  4. Set the tear-off. One old layer is included in the base rate. A second layer adds $0.75 per sq ft for the extra removal and disposal.
  5. Read the range, then the permit note. Your total is a low–high range with the permit fee shown as its own line — and the permit panel explains your city's actual rule, because in several cities a standard re-roof needs no permit at all.

What Moves a Roof Quote

  • Material choice. Nothing swings the total like material. On a 2,300 sq ft roof, moving from architectural shingle to metal can add five figures; 3-tab is the budget option but typically carries a shorter lifespan and warranty.
  • Local labor rates. Roofing is labor-heavy, so metro wage levels drive the per-square-foot rate — the main reason the same roof costs roughly $9,100–$14,400 in Charlotte and $18,600–$24,950 in New York at the settings above.
  • Pitch and complexity. Steep pitches, multiple valleys, dormers, skylights and chimneys all slow the crew down and add flashing work. The calculator's pitch factor covers the area effect; unusual complexity pushes bids toward the top of the range.
  • Deck condition. Rotten or sagging decking is only visible after tear-off. Contractors charge per replaced sheet, so a contingency of a few hundred dollars is realistic on older homes.
  • Code upgrades. Hurricane zones, snow-load regions and wildfire areas can require upgraded underlayment, fasteners or ventilation that a basic quote elsewhere never includes.

Permit Rules Worth Knowing

Permit rules are the part national calculators usually skip — and they are genuinely local. A few from the cities in this calculator:

  • Chicago: no building permit for a residential re-roof at a pitch of 2:12 or steeper on buildings up to 4 stories; low-slope or structural work is a different story ($475 per area up to 5,000 sq ft from January 2027).
  • Phoenix & Seattle: a like-for-like re-roof with the same material typically needs no permit; changing material or structure triggers one.
  • Austin: asphalt shingles replacing asphalt shingles are exempt, as is any covering that does not affect the structure; when a permit is needed, the Express Permit runs about $87 plus $136 plan review.
  • Miami: a permit is always required, and Miami-Dade is a High-Velocity Hurricane Zone — every component needs a Notice of Acceptance product approval, with wind-code inspections on top.
  • New York: covering replacement above the deck is often exempt as an ordinary repair (1 RCNY 101-14); replace deck or structure and a DOB filing applies, with fees of $200–$800.
  • Los Angeles: a permit is required, but LADBS issues most re-roofs as Express Permits with no plan check; the fee is valuation-based, about $284 on a $12,000 job.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this roofing cost calculator really free?

Yes — completely free, with no sign-up and no quote requests sold to contractors. Everything runs in your browser: your home details never leave your device, and nobody will call you because you used it.

How accurate is the estimate?

It is a researched range, not a bid. Each city's rate comes from published contractor pricing and municipal fee schedules (researched November 2026), and published sources disagree by ±20–30% for the same city — which is exactly why the calculator always shows a low–high range. Deck condition, roof access and code upgrades can move a real bid within, or occasionally beyond, the range.

Do I need a permit to replace my roof?

It depends entirely on your city. Chicago exempts residential re-roofs at a 2:12 pitch or steeper, Phoenix and Seattle exempt like-for-like replacements, and Austin exempts asphalt-for-asphalt swaps — while Miami and Houston always require a permit. Pick your city in the calculator and the permit panel shows its actual rule and typical fee.

What is the cheapest roofing material?

3-tab asphalt shingle is the cheapest common option — in this calculator it prices at 0.78× the architectural shingle rate, roughly 22% less. Architectural shingle is the mid-point most quotes are built on; metal runs about 1.9× and tile about 2.3× the architectural price, though both last considerably longer.

Why does the same roof cost so much more in New York than in Charlotte?

Mostly labor and code. Roofing is labor-heavy, and New York metro crew rates are among the highest in the country, while Charlotte's are among the lowest of the cities tracked here. Local rules add the rest: hurricane product approvals in Miami, valuation-based permit fees in Los Angeles and Denver, and snow or wind requirements all push bids up or down.
