Hashtags are still the simplest way to put a post in front of people who do not follow you yet — if you pick the right mix. How to Use

  1. Type your topic — two or three plain words is plenty: “funny cat videos”, “road trip”, “home workout”. The generator reads your words to detect the category (animals, travel, food, fitness, fashion, business, pets, gaming, motivation or nature) and also builds extra tags from your exact topic.
  2. Choose your platform — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or YouTube. Platform tweaks are automatic: TikTok sets swap in #fyp and #foryoupage, and YouTube sets lead with #shorts and #youtubeshorts.
  3. Click “Generate hashtags.” You get three labelled groups — Big reach (8 tags), Medium reach (9 tags) and Niche (8 tags) — 25 hashtags in total, each group with a note on what it is for.
  4. Copy what you need. Every group has its own Copy button, and the “Copy all” button grabs the full set of 25 in one tap, ready to paste.
  5. Paste and trim. Drop the tags under your caption or into the first comment. If the post looks crowded, keep the whole Niche group, keep most of Medium, and cut a few Big-reach tags first — the big ones expire fastest anyway.

Hashtag Tips From Creators Who Grow Steadily

  • Mix reach levels on purpose. Big tags like #viral sit under millions of posts, so you get seconds of visibility — treat them as lottery tickets. Medium tags keep you discoverable for hours. Niche tags may have only thousands of posts, but the people browsing them are looking for exactly your content, and they follow accounts. Growth comes from the mix, not from 25 giant tags.
  • Never paste the identical set on every post. Repeating one block of hashtags word-for-word for weeks looks automated and can limit distribution. Because this generator rebuilds the set from each topic, vary your topics' wording and your set stays naturally varied too.
  • Match quantity to the platform. Instagram rewards roughly 5–15 well-chosen tags. Facebook posts perform best with just 2–5. TikTok cares more about the keywords in your description than tag volume. YouTube Shorts need only 3–5, ideally including #shorts. Generate 25, then use the slice each platform actually rewards.
  • Glance at a tag before you trust it. Open any unfamiliar hashtag once and check that the posts under it match your content and your audience. Tags occasionally get hijacked by spam or restricted by the platform — a ten-second check protects your post.
  • Put your most specific tag first in the niche group. The generator already builds tags from your exact topic words (for example #funnycatvideos from “funny cat videos”). Those hyper-specific tags are where your first loyal followers hide — never trim them to save space.
  • Review monthly, not daily. Check your post insights once a month and note which posts reached non-followers. If niche-heavy sets keep winning, lean further into niche tags; if a medium tag keeps appearing in your best posts, build future sets around it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hashtags should I actually use?

It depends on the platform. Instagram: 5–15 focused tags beat 30 random ones. Facebook: 2–5 is the sweet spot. TikTok: a handful, with your energy spent on description keywords instead. YouTube Shorts: 3–5 tags including #shorts. This generator gives you 25 sorted by reach precisely so you can take the right slice for each platform.

Are the generated hashtags safe to use?

The generator draws from a curated bank of mainstream, widely used hashtags for each category, plus tags built from your own topic words. No banned or adult tags are included. As with any hashtag tool, glance at a tag's page once if it is new to you — platforms occasionally restrict tags without warning.

Should hashtags go in the caption or the first comment?

On Instagram both placements work, and reach is essentially the same — the first comment simply keeps your caption cleaner. On TikTok and Facebook, put them in the caption or description itself, because comments carry less weight there. On YouTube, place them in the description or title of the Short.

Why 25 hashtags and not 30?

Twenty-five is enough to cover all three reach levels properly — 8 big, 9 medium, 8 niche — without encouraging tag stuffing. More tags do not mean more reach; relevance does. A tight, topic-matched set of 25 (or the smaller slice your platform rewards) consistently outperforms a padded list of 30.

Can I reuse the same hashtag set for every post?

You can, but you should not. Platforms look for relevance per post, and identical repeated blocks can appear spammy. Generate a fresh set per post from that post's actual topic — it takes five seconds and keeps every set specific, which is exactly what the algorithms reward.
